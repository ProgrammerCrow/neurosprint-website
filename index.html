Scientific Background

The Power of Cognitive Inhibition

In 1935, psychologist John Ridley Stroop discovered that reading a word is an automatic, involuntary brain process, while naming its ink color requires deliberate executive control.

When color and meaning clash (e.g. the word "BLUE" written in red ink), your prefrontal cortex must actively suppress the automatic reading response. Regularly training this mental interference builds neuroplasticity, working memory, and rapid decision-making under pressure.
